### C — Medium

  - The canonical medium LLD: it assumes the state modeling TicTacToe teaches and adds Lot-to-Floor-to-Spot composition, enum-to-enum allocation, ticket lifecycle, fee strategy and singleton concurrency — the most-asked LLD question in senior and staff loops.

## Interview readiness

This folder holds two files totalling ~300 lines, and only one of them is really a design: ParkingLotExample.java is a respectable single-file model (Vehicle/Spot/Floor/Lot/Ticket, enum state, one validated entry point, coarse `synchronized`), while TicTacToe.java is LeetCode 348's incremental win-checker wearing a game's name — there is no Board, Player or Game object, no turn enforcement, no invalid-move rejection and no draw detection, so it will not survive an interviewer asking "now let me play two games concurrently" or "add a Player abstraction". Measured against what actually gets asked, he has exactly one of the ~8 canonical archetypes covered: resource allocation (parking lot). He has no problem driven by a scheduling decision (elevator), none with a real concurrency story beyond a coarse lock (seat booking with holds and expiry), none with business-rule modelling and a ledger (Splitwise), none where behaviour lives in state objects rather than an enum flag (vending machine/ATM), none with polymorphic rule validation across a type hierarchy (chess), and none with one-to-many event fan-out (notifications). The Design-Patterns folder is complete on all 23 GoF patterns, which is good, but patterns-in-isolation is the easy half — the interview tests whether he reaches for Strategy or State *under time pressure on an unfamiliar domain*, and note that even in ParkingLot the pricing and spot-selection "seams" are still inline code, not interfaces. Realistically he is prepared for one specific 45-minute prompt and would be improvising on any of the other five most common ones; four problems (elevator, seat booking, Splitwise, rate limiter) would take him from one archetype to five and cover the large majority of what a senior/staff LLD round actually draws from.

| medium | Chess (or any piece-hierarchy board game) | Polymorphic move validation — Piece.canMove(board, from, to) overridden per piece — with Board as a real object, Move objects recorded in a history for undo, and game-termination checks kept separate from move application. | His TicTacToe is a win-checker, not an object model: no Board, Player or Game class, no turn enforcement, no rejection of an occupied cell, no draw.
