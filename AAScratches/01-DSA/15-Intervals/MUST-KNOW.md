# 15-Intervals — must-know order

**Techniques in this topic:** Sort by start, then scan: the three primitives are detect overlap, merge overlap, and count overlap — every file here is one of those three wearing a costume, Chronological sweep with two independently sorted start/end arrays: the single idea behind minimum platforms, meeting rooms II, min groups, and max population year, Difference array / event deltas: turn each interval into a +x at start and -x at end, sort by position, prefix-sum to get concurrency or load at every point, Greedy sort by END time (activity selection): the opposite sort key from merging — it maximizes kept non-overlapping intervals and minimizes the arrows/groups needed, Two-pointer walk over two already-sorted interval lists: intersection is [max(starts), min(ends)], and you always advance the pointer with the smaller end, Heap-based resource assignment: when the question stops asking HOW MANY rooms and starts asking WHICH room, a counting sweep is no longer enough and you need two priority queues, Recognition over invention: the interview skill being trained is spotting that car pooling, train platforms, population years, and conference rooms are the same problem

## Full practice order


### A — Building blocks

- `A01_MeetingRooms.java` — Sort by start, adjacent overlap check
  - The overlap predicate itself — prev.end > cur.start — which every other file in the folder assumes you can write without thinking; too easy to be a must-know on its own, but nothing else makes sense before it.
- `A02_MergeIntervals.java` — Sort by start, merge into accumulator **[must-know]**
  - Turns the overlap check from #1 into the canonical union algorithm; this is THE interval pattern and the parent of Insert Interval, and it is asked at every level constantly.
- `A03_MinimumPlatforms.java` — Two sorted arrays, chronological sweep **[must-know]**
  - Third and final primitive: decouple starts from ends, sort each independently, walk time forward counting concurrency — unlocks Meeting Rooms II/III, Min Groups, Car Pooling and Max Population Year all at once.

### B — Easy

- `B01_MaximumPopulationYear.java` — Difference array over bounded domain
  - The only genuinely easy problem here, and it earns its slot by carrying both solutions — the sweep from A3 and the +1/-1 bucket difference array that Car Pooling later generalizes to weights.

### C — Medium

- `C01_InsertInterval.java` — Three-phase scan on sorted list **[must-know]**
  - The direct sequel to Merge Intervals — same merge logic but the input is pre-sorted, so it becomes before/overlap/after in O(n); asked as the follow-up to merge often enough that you must have the three-phase skeleton memorised.
- `C02_IntervalListIntersections.java` — Two-pointer intersection of sorted lists
  - Placed right after the merge family because it is the mirror image: intersection is max(starts), min(ends), and advancing the smaller end is the two-pointer habit reused later.
- `C03_MeetingRoomsII.java` — Max concurrent intervals count
  - Literally Minimum Platforms under the name interviewers actually say, so owning A3 already covers it — its job here is name recognition plus knowing the heap-of-end-times alternative that Meeting Rooms III needs.
- `C04_DivideIntervalsIntoMinGroups.java` — Min groups = max overlap depth
  - Pure recognition drill placed after the two rooms/platforms files: the insight is only that 'fewest groups with no internal overlap' equals 'peak concurrency', so the algorithm is already known.
- `C05_CarPooling.java` — Weighted event deltas, sorted sweep
  - Generalises the +1/-1 counting of A3 and B1 to arbitrary passenger weights against a capacity ceiling; must come after the unweighted versions or the delta idea has nothing to build on.
- `C06_NonOverlappingIntervals.java` — Greedy: sort by end time
  - Opens the second axis of the folder — sorting by END rather than start — and proves the classic activity-selection exchange argument; everything before it sorted by start, so it belongs late.
- `C07_MinimumArrowsToBurstBalloons.java` — Greedy grouping by end time
  - Same sort-by-end greedy as #6 with the answer reframed from 'intervals removed' to 'groups kept', which makes it the cheapest possible confirmation that the greedy actually generalises.

### D — Hard

- `D01_MeetingRoomsIII.java` — Two heaps, delayed scheduling
  - The only hard file: the question shifts from how many rooms to which room, so counting collapses and you need an idle-room min-heap by index, a busy-room heap by (endTime, index), delayed start times and long overflow care.

## Interview readiness

This is a strong folder for the offline/batch half of interval questions and would carry him through a standard 45-minute interval round without trouble: he has all three of his stated primitives (detect, merge, count), both sort keys (by start for merging, by end for activity-selection), the two-sorted-arrays chronological sweep, the difference-array/event-delta form, the two-pointer walk over two sorted lists, and the heap-assignment upgrade in MeetingRoomsIII that answers "which room" rather than "how many". Cross-folder coverage is better than the file list suggests — weighted interval scheduling (LC 1235) sits in 01-DSA\12-Dynamic-Programming\ImportantJobSchedulingMaxProfit.java, minimum interval cover in 01-DSA\13-Greedy\MinimumNumberOfTapsToOpenToWaterAGarden.java, greedy-plus-heap event attendance in 01-DSA\08-Heap-Priority-Queue\MaximumNumberofEventsThatCanBeAttended.java, and Partition Labels and Exclusive Time of Functions in the Greedy and Stack folders — so those are practiced, just not linked from here, which is a recall risk under pressure and worth fixing with a README cross-reference. The real weakness is structural and shows up in exactly two interviewer follow-ups. First, every single file is offline: the whole array is handed to him and he sorts it. The moment bookings arrive one at a time (book(start, end)) or queries land at arbitrary points, sorting is off the table and he needs a TreeMap with floorKey/ceilingKey or an offline binary search — and a grep over the entire 01-DSA tree shows only one file uses floorKey/ceilingKey at all (TimeBasedKeyValueStore.java), and it is not an interval problem. Second, his three primitives are missing a fourth: subtract. Nothing in the repo computes the gaps in a union or removes a range from a set of ranges, which is why Employee Free Time — the most-asked interval hard at Meta and Google — is the single highest-value thing absent here. Fix the online/TreeMap gap and the complement/subtract gap and this folder is genuinely senior-complete; leave them and a competent interviewer finds the edge in one follow-up.

## Gaps — canonical problems NOT in this folder

| Priority | Problem | Technique | Why it matters |
|---|---|---|---|
| high | Employee Free Time (LC 759) | Merge k sorted interval lists (heap or flatten-and-sort), then return the COMPLEMENT — the gaps between merged blocks, excluding the infinite ends. | This is the canonical interval hard at Meta and Google and the biggest single hole here. His themes list three primitives — detect, merge, count — and the fourth one, subtract/complement, appears in no file in the folder. MergeIntervals produces the union but never asks what is left over between the blocks, and inverting that is where people freeze: the off-by-one on gap boundaries, dropping the interval before the first block and after the last, and handling the case where two blocks touch exactly (end == start) so there is no gap at all. It also layers the k-way merge on top, which is the natural staff follow-up ('now there are 10,000 employees, do not flatten 10 million intervals into one array — use a min-heap of list heads'). He has the merge and he has heaps; he has never had to compose them and then invert the result. |
| high | My Calendar I / II / III (LC 729 / 731 / 732) | Online interval insertion with a balanced BST (Java TreeMap): floorKey/ceilingKey to detect overlap in O(log n) for I, a second 'double-booked' list or delta map for II, and a +1/-1 delta TreeMap with a running prefix max for III. | Every problem in this folder is batch — the full array arrives, he sorts it, he sweeps. The standard senior follow-up to MeetingRooms is 'bookings now arrive one at a time over an API, support book(start, end)', and at that point sorting is dead because you cannot re-sort on every call. The required move is a TreeMap keyed by start where you check the predecessor's end and the successor's start. Only one file in the entire 01-DSA tree touches floorKey/ceilingKey (TimeBasedKeyValueStore.java) and it is not an interval problem, so this technique is effectively untrained. My Calendar III is especially valuable because it is MeetingRoomsII/MaximumPopulationYear re-asked in streaming form: the same +1/-1 delta idea he already knows, but held in a TreeMap so the prefix-sum can be recomputed incrementally instead of over a pre-sorted array. Getting to see his own technique survive the change of setting is exactly the 'recognition over invention' skill his themes say he is training. |
| high | Number of Flowers in Full Bloom (LC 2251), with Minimum Interval to Include Each Query (LC 1851) as the harder twin | Offline query processing: sort starts and ends into two arrays and, for each query point, binary search both (count of started minus count of ended); or sort queries and intervals together and sweep with a heap for 1851. | His chronological-sweep theme answers concurrency AT event boundaries only. This asks concurrency at arbitrary query points, which is a different computation and a very common interview twist — 'how many meetings were running at 3:47pm', 'how many servers were live at these 10,000 timestamps'. The trap is that the naive answer is O(n*q) and the interviewer is specifically checking whether he sees that the two independently sorted start/end arrays he already builds for MinimumPlatforms are directly binary-searchable, which drops it to O((n+q) log n). It is the smallest conceptual leap on this list and the highest ratio of interview frequency to study time. LC 1851 is the same setup one level up — sort queries offline and use a min-heap by interval length — and is a recurring Google question. |
| medium | Remove Interval (LC 1272), then Range Module (LC 715) | Interval subtraction with splitting: removing a range from a covering interval can produce zero, one, or two pieces; Range Module maintains that under add/remove/query with a TreeMap. | Insertion he has (InsertInterval); deletion he does not, anywhere. Removal is materially harder than insertion because one existing interval can split into two, and the four cases (no overlap, clipped left, clipped right, split in the middle) are where candidates lose the whiteboard. Range Module is the staff-level form and is a known Google and Meta question: it is My Calendar plus deletion plus a queryRange that must confirm full coverage, not just any overlap — that last distinction (covered-entirely vs overlaps-at-all) is a classic trip-up. Start with 1272 for the case analysis, then do 715 once the TreeMap problems above are solid. |
| medium | Data Stream as Disjoint Intervals / SummaryRanges (LC 352) | Online merge into a disjoint set of intervals — TreeMap keyed by start, on addNum check the floor entry for an extend-right and the ceiling entry for an extend-left, merging both neighbours when the new value closes a gap. | This is MergeIntervals as a streaming design problem and it is asked as a design-flavoured round at Google and Amazon. The specific skill it trains and the folder does not is the three-way merge on insert: a single new point can extend the interval on its left, extend the one on its right, or fuse the two into one, and handling only the first two cases is the standard wrong answer that passes small tests. It also pairs naturally with the My Calendar work above — same data structure, different invariant (disjoint-and-maximal rather than merely non-overlapping), and being able to say out loud why the invariant differs is a senior-level signal. |
| medium | The Skyline Problem (LC 218) | Sweep line over sorted x-events with an active-set max structure — a TreeMap<height, count> multiset or a max-heap with lazy deletion — emitting a key point whenever the running max height changes. | This is the hard everyone expects in the interval bucket at staff level, and it is the only interval problem that forces a sweep carrying a removable maximum rather than a running counter. His CarPooling and MeetingRoomsII sweeps only ever add and subtract an integer; here the sweep must remove an arbitrary element from the middle of the active set, which is the technique step up. The interview value is not just the answer — it is the tie-breaking discussion (starts before ends at the same x, taller start first, shorter end first) and the reason a plain PriorityQueue needs lazy deletion or a counted TreeMap. Less frequently asked than the items above, but when it appears it is usually the deciding round, and he currently has no path into it. |
| medium | Remove Covered Intervals (LC 1288) | Sort by start ascending and by end DESCENDING on ties, then a single scan tracking the max end seen so far. | Cheap to add and it isolates a subtlety his current files never punish him for: the tie-break in the comparator. In MergeIntervals, ties on start do not matter because the intervals get merged anyway, so a comparator of (a[0] - b[0]) is fine. Here, [1,4] and [1,2] with the wrong tie-break makes [1,4] look covered by nothing and [1,2] get counted, and the answer is silently wrong on a case the candidate will not think to test. It is also a common Amazon and Google warm-up, and it gives him a crisp thing to say about why the comparator matters — an easy senior-signal moment in the first five minutes of a round. Also worth noting the overflow-safe form: use Integer.compare rather than subtraction, which MergeIntervals.java currently does not. |
| low | Right Interval (LC 436) | For each interval find the one with the smallest start >= its end — binary search over a sorted array of (start, originalIndex), or a TreeMap with ceilingEntry. | Low cost, and it trains the successor lookup as its own primitive in both flavours (array binary search and TreeMap ceiling), which is the exact lookup every online interval problem above depends on. It also forces him to carry original indices through a sort, which is a routine interview requirement his current files never demand because they all return merged values rather than references to the input. Genuinely lower priority than the rest of this list — do it only after the TreeMap and complement gaps are closed, or skip it if time is short. |
